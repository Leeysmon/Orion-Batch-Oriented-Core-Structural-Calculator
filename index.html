Orion – Batch Oriented Core Structural Calculator

True plane orientations for many oriented-core measurements, from uploaded collar, survey and structure tables

Runs locally – files never leave your computer
Step 1

Upload data tables

Each table can be a .csv or .xlsx file. Drag a file onto a box or click “Choose file”.

ⓘ Help
  • The first row of each file must be the column headings.
  • Collar: one row per hole (ID, X, Y, Z, maximum depth). Survey: one row per downhole survey station (ID, depth, azimuth, dip). Structures: one row per oriented measurement (ID, depth, alpha, beta, attributes).
  • The Hole ID must be spelled the same in all three files (not case-sensitive).
  • Files are read in your browser only; nothing is uploaded.

Collar

Hole ID, collar X / Y / Z, maximum depth

Survey

Hole ID, azimuth, dip and depth of each survey station

Structures

Hole ID, depth, alpha, beta and descriptive attributes
Step 2

Identify columns

Match the columns in each file to the fields the calculator needs. Likely matches are pre-selected; fields marked * are required.

ⓘ Help
  • Pick the column that holds each field. Numeric fields (depth, angles, coordinates) are checked, and a warning appears if the chosen column contains text.
  • Survey dip sign: choose whether a downward hole is stored as negative (−60) or positive (60).
  • Structure depth: choose whether mDepth is the top or bottom of the structure. If your file has a per-row Top/Bottom column, map it to “Top/Bottom marker” and it overrides this setting.
  • Core length (cm) is used with the top/bottom setting to find the structure’s mid-depth, where the plane is placed. Without it, the mDepth itself is used.
  • Alpha is 0–90°; Beta is 0–360°, measured clockwise looking down-hole from the bottom-of-hole line.
Load a file in Step 1 to map its columns.
Step 3

Calculated structures

ⓘ Help
  • Filters drive the viewers: as soon as you type a filter, the 3D viewer and the stereonet show only the rows that pass the filters. The viewers' own settings (hole, depth interval, legend) can then narrow that set further, but never widen it.
  • Filter boxes sit under each column heading. Text matches anywhere in the cell and is not case-sensitive. Filters in different columns are combined (AND).
  • Several values in one column (OR): separate them with | or a comma – e.g. stringer | vn in Type, or quartz, carbonate in Vein Min. 1. Add ! in front to exclude a value, e.g. !stringer.
  • Numbers accept >10, <=5, =3, !=0 or a range 10..20; these can also be combined with |, e.g. <50 | >300.
  • Click a column heading to sort (ascending → descending → off).
  • Tick rows (or the header box to tick every listed row) and press Show in viewer to limit the viewers to just those rows. Show all in viewer removes that limit; Clear sort & filters resets the filters.
  • Click a row to select it; clicking a plane in 3D or a line/pole on the stereonet selects and scrolls to its row.
Step 4

Results & 3D preview

ⓘ Help
  • Rotate by dragging, pan by holding the left and right mouse buttons together and dragging, zoom with the wheel. Click a plane (or a table row) to see its strike/dip.
  • Two-tone planes: the normal colour is the hanging-wall (upper) side; a darker tone shows when you view the foot-wall (lower) side.
  • Legend: click an entry to hide or show those structures (also filters the stereonet). Outlines are solid on the hanging-wall side and dotted on the foot-wall side.
  • Depth interval limits planes to a depth range along the selected hole(s). Axes show X (East), Y (North) and Z (elevation) in the same units as your collar file.
  • Maximise enlarges the view; Export saves it as PNG, JPEG or PDF. Strike uses the right-hand rule (dip is to the right of strike).
  • Compass & plane: teal = strike line, orange arrow = dip direction, black = hole direction at that depth.

Step 4 · Stereonet

Stereonet projection

ⓘ Help
  • Equal-area (Schmidt) lower-hemisphere net. Each structure is a great circle (the plane) and/or a pole (the normal to the plane). Colours match the 3D viewer.
  • It always shows the same structures as the 3D viewer: table filters (Step 3), any rows sent with Show in viewer, and the hole, depth interval and legend toggles.
  • The dashed red line is the mean plane of everything currently shown (poles are aligned to one hemisphere, averaged and normalised); the red ✕ is its pole.
  • Pole-density contours shade clustering of the poles from light blue (sparse) to deep blue (dense), with contour lines at 25–85 % of the peak density.
  • Click a line or pole to select it – the matching table row is highlighted and scrolled into view. Use the Stereonet settings to change line thickness, point size and what is drawn.
